Archana Vaidheeswaran

AI evaluation and safety research · Program Director, Algoverse

I study how to check AI systems. My papers ask whether a model's reasoning holds together, and whether a model used as a judge actually checks what it is asked to check.

I write most of my papers with the early-career researchers I mentor. I am Program Director at Algoverse, an AI safety research fellowship, and I started PRISM, a peer-vetted research fellowship. From 2017 to 2022 I worked in industry as an AI research engineer and data scientist.

I served on the board of Women in Machine Learning (WiML) from 2023 to 2026. Before that I organised the WiML Un-Workshop at ICML 2021 as Virtual Experience Chair.

I have an M.Sc. in Computer Engineering by research from the National University of Singapore.

Evaluating AI judges

When a model is used to judge, verify or explain, does it check what it is asked to check?

  • ICML 2026FAGEN workshop
    Triggers and Diagnostics for LLM-Based Interpretability Failures in Active Inference Agents
    Archana Vaidheeswaran, Param Raval, Rohit Shenoy

    An audit of an LLM that explains an autonomous agent's decisions on German energy grid data. None of 30 explanations flagged an injected 600 MW false reading. All three models justified wrong actions 80–95% of the time.

    First authorOpenReview
  • EACL 2026Student Research Workshop
    FluffInjector: Diagnosing Logical Consistency Failures in Chain-of-Thought Reward Models
    Author list on OpenReview

    A benchmark where 25–40% of reasoning steps are swapped for plausible filler. Frontier judges accept these chains far too often. SmartRM, the verifier we trained on it, cuts false positives from 37.4% to 2.7%.

    41.6% acceptanceOpenReview
  • WACV 2026VALED workshop
    Quantifying Text Dominance in Multimodal AI
    Author list on OpenReview

    Vision-language models proposed for copyright disputes follow the prompt over the image. Primed with a legal accusation, they rate unrelated images as similar. We measure this with a Blindness Index.

    OpenReview

Consistent reasoning

Does a model's reasoning hold together across repeated samples, negations and hidden steps?

  • EMNLP 2026Industry Track
    CAST: Contrastive Adjudication via Selective Triggering for Clinical LLMs
    Krishna Senthilkumar, Archana Vaidheeswaran, Shivank Garg

    When a clinical LLM gives different answers to the same question, CAST puts the competing arguments side by side and asks the model to settle them. On PubMedQA with GPT-4o-mini, accuracy on those questions rises from 28.0% to 57.0%.

    PosterPaper link to come
  • ICML 2026FoGen workshop
    Interpreting Latent CoT Reasoning as Dynamical Systems
    Shreya Sanjay Boyane, Sabari Iyyappan Duraipandian, Manju Nagesh, Jerome Francis, Archana Vaidheeswaran, Kevin Zhu

    We treat hidden reasoning tokens as a path through representation space. CODI settles like a stable attractor. COCONUT expands like an unstable system.

    OpenReviewarXiv
  • KDD 2026SeT-LLM workshop
    Mutual Information Transfer Regularization for Logical Consistency: A Controlled Empirical Study
    Author list on OpenReview

    We tested whether a layer-level regulariser stops QA models giving the same answer to a question and its negation. Across three backbones and five seeds the gains were small and unstable. Training directly on negated examples works better.

    Negative resultPosterOpenReview

Pluralism and many agents

What does alignment do to the range of views a model holds, across languages and across populations of agents?

  • ICML 2026Forecasting workshop
    Preference Optimization Drives Monoculture in LLM Prediction Markets
    James Begin, Brendan Gho, Suman Muppavarapu, Tyson Tsay, Atharva Mohan, Afnan Shaik, Ruizhe Li, Vasu Sharma, Archana Vaidheeswaran

    Prediction markets work when traders think independently. Preference-optimised LLM traders drift toward a shared prior, and the market prices confidently and wrongly.

    OpenReviewarXiv
  • ICLR 2026CAO workshop
    Cross-Lingual Fairness Drift in LLM Moral Reasoning
    Author list on OpenReview

    Five models, 50 moral dilemmas and four languages: English, Spanish, Korean and Mandarin. Consequentialist bias grows outside English, and cultural grounding drops by up to 88%.

    OpenReview
  • 2025Preprint
    From Competition to Coordination: Market Making as a Scalable Framework for Safe and Aligned Multi-Agent LLM Systems
    Brendan Gho, Suman Muppavarapu, Afnan Shaik, Tyson Tsay, James Begin, Kevin Zhu, Archana Vaidheeswaran, Vasu Sharma

    A market maker as a coordination mechanism that keeps a population of LLM agents calibrated as it grows.

    arXiv
